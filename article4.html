Improve Your Credit Score Before Applying for a Mortgage

Your credit score is one of the most important factors lenders consider when approving your mortgage. In 2025, with lending standards tighter than ever, a good credit score could mean a lower interest rate and more favorable terms.

1. Check Your Credit Report

Start by requesting a free copy of your credit report. Review it for accuracy and dispute any errors that may be dragging your score down.

2. Pay Down Credit Card Debt

High credit utilization can hurt your score. Aim to keep your credit card balances below 30% of your total credit limit.

3. Avoid Opening New Accounts

Each credit inquiry can lower your score temporarily. Try not to apply for new loans or cards in the months leading up to your mortgage application.

4. Pay Bills on Time

Timely payments make up a significant portion of your credit score. Set up reminders or auto-pay to avoid missing deadlines.

5. Increase Your Credit Limit

Ask your current credit card providers for a limit increase to help improve your utilization ratio—just don’t spend the extra credit!

6. Be Patient and Consistent

Credit improvement takes time. Start preparing 6–12 months before applying for a mortgage to see meaningful changes in your score.

A higher credit score can save you thousands over the life of your loan, so start improving it today.
