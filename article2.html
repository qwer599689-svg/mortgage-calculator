Fixed vs Variable Mortgage Rates: Which Is Right for You?

When buying a home in 2025, one of the biggest decisions you'll face is whether to choose a fixed or variable mortgage rate. Each option has its own benefits and risks, and the right choice depends on your financial situation, risk tolerance, and future plans.

What Is a Fixed Mortgage Rate?

A fixed mortgage rate means your interest rate—and therefore your monthly payment—stays the same for the entire loan term (e.g., 15 or 30 years). It offers stability and predictability, making it easier to plan your finances.

  • Pros: Stable payments, protects against rate hikes
  • Cons: Typically higher initial rate than variable options

What Is a Variable Mortgage Rate?

With a variable mortgage rate, your interest rate can change based on market conditions. These mortgages often start with a lower rate, but your monthly payment can increase or decrease over time.

  • Pros: Lower initial payments, potential savings if rates drop
  • Cons: Risk of rising rates, less predictability

Which One Should You Choose?

If you value consistency and want to lock in a stable rate, a fixed mortgage may be better. However, if you're financially flexible and willing to take some risk for potential savings, a variable mortgage could work in your favor.

In 2025, with interest rates potentially shifting, many borrowers are taking a hybrid approach—starting with a variable rate and refinancing later.

Final Thoughts

Ultimately, your choice should align with your long-term financial goals. Speak with a mortgage advisor to compare options based on your income, lifestyle, and market outlook.
